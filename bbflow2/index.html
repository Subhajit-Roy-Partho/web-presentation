IDPFlow: Flow Matching for Intrinsically Disordered Protein Ensembles

Generating conformational ensembles without an equilibrium structure — extending backbone flow matching to the disordered proteome.

IDP dynamics SE(3) flow matching Sequence-conditioned No equilibrium structure Disease applications
Research proposal for NSF MCB. Background: Wolf et al. arXiv:2503.05738 (BBFlow); Dyson & Wright, Nat. Rev. Mol. Cell Biol. 2005; Tesei et al. Nature 2024.

Background 1

Proteins: sequence, structure, and motion

Proteins are linear polymers of 20 amino acids that fold into three-dimensional structures and perform virtually every cellular function. Structure determines function — but function also depends on dynamics.

\[p(\mathbf{x}) \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{E(\mathbf{x})}{k_B T}\right)\]

At physiological temperature, a protein does not sit in one conformation. It samples an ensemble of structures weighted by the Boltzmann distribution. The lower the energy \(E(\mathbf{x})\), the more often that conformation is visited.

Key insight for classmates: the biologically relevant object is not a single 3D structure — it is a probability distribution over structures. Generating this ensemble is the computational challenge we address.
Boltzmann distribution and protein dynamics: Adcock & McCammon, Chem. Rev. 2006; Karplus & Kuriyan, PNAS 2005.

Background 2

Intrinsically disordered proteins — the flexible 30%

Not all proteins fold into a stable 3D structure. Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) and regions (IDRs) lack persistent secondary or tertiary structure under physiological conditions, yet they are biologically active.

Prevalence

~30–40% of the human proteome contains long disordered regions. >28,000 IDRs catalogued in IDRome (Tesei 2024).

Function

Hub proteins, transcription factors, signaling mediators. Disorder enables promiscuous binding and rapid regulation.

The challenge

No crystal structure. AlphaFold predictions are unreliable for IDRs. MD is expensive (µs timescales needed).

Why now? The IDRome dataset (2024) provides 28,058 coarse-grained MD trajectories of human IDPs — for the first time making large-scale ML training feasible.
IDP prevalence: Uversky, Chem. Rev. 2014; IDRome dataset: Tesei et al. Nature 2024; disorder prediction: PONDR, IUPred3.

Motivation

Disordered regions drive cancer, neurodegeneration, and more

Many of the most medically important proteins are intrinsically disordered. Their disorder is essential both for function and for disease contribution.

Cancer

p53 TAD (residues 1–73): disordered N-terminal activation domain. Mutated or inactivated in ~50% of all human cancers.

c-Myc: drives proliferation via disordered activation domain.

Neurodegeneration

α-synuclein: natively disordered, forms amyloid fibrils in Parkinson's disease.

Tau: mostly disordered, aggregates in Alzheimer's disease and other tauopathies.

Other diseases

IAPP (islet amyloid polypeptide): aggregates in Type 2 diabetes.

FUS / TDP-43: low-complexity IDRs involved in ALS and FTD.

Drug discovery bottleneck: IDPs are often called "undruggable" because their transient binding sites are invisible in static crystal structures. Ensemble prediction can unlock these sites.
IDP disease relevance: Uversky et al., Science 2008; p53 disorder: Lee et al., J. Mol. Biol. 2000; α-synuclein: Bhatt et al., Nature Struct. Mol. Biol. 2021.

Background 3

How we study IDP ensembles — and why it is hard

Both experimental and computational approaches struggle with IDPs. The challenge is that disorder means no stable endpoint: methods must sample a broad, flat energy landscape.

  • NMR: rich ensemble information via chemical shifts and NOEs. But expensive, size-limited (<50 kDa), and cannot fully determine the ensemble alone.
  • SAXS: fast, gives global compactness (Rg). Low resolution; cannot distinguish individual conformations.
  • Molecular Dynamics (MD): gold standard for ensemble accuracy, but IDPs require µs–ms timescales vs. ns for folded proteins — 100–1,000× more expensive.
  • ML for folded proteins: BBFlow, AlphaFlow, ConfDiff — accurate but require an equilibrium backbone structure as input. Fail for IDPs.
The gap: there is no fast, sequence-only computational method that generates accurate IDP conformational ensembles. IDPFlow is proposed to fill this gap.
Experimental IDP methods: Mittag & Forman-Kay, Curr. Opin. Struct. Biol. 2007; CALVADOS2: Löhr et al. JCTC 2022; BBFlow: Wolf et al. 2025.

Background 4

BBFlow: backbone geometry as conditioning signal

BBFlow (Wolf et al., NeurIPS 2025) is the current state-of-the-art for fast conformational ensemble generation of folded proteins. Understanding it is essential to motivate our extension.

BBFlow's two key innovations:

1

Conditional flow matching: frame the Boltzmann distribution as \(p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{x}_{eq})\) — conformations conditioned on the equilibrium structure. The SE(3) flow vector field is learned to transport a prior to this target.

2

Conditional prior: the starting noise distribution is not pure Gaussian but is biased toward \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\) via geodesic interpolation on SE(3). This dramatically improves training efficiency.

\[p_0(\mathbf{x}_0|\mathbf{x}_{eq}) = \text{geodesic}(\mathbf{x}_{eq},\, \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),\, s)\]

Result: >100× faster than MD at comparable accuracy. But the model requires \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\) as input.

BBFlow: Wolf, Seute et al. arXiv:2503.05738, NeurIPS 2025; SE(3) flow matching: Yim et al. ICML 2023 (FrameFlow); Flow matching: Lipman et al. ICLR 2023.

Key Problem

BBFlow fails for IDPs — and why that matters

BBFlow's conditioning mechanism is fundamentally tied to the existence of a stable equilibrium structure \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\). For IDPs, this assumption breaks down completely.

Folded protein

Deep energy minimum. Crystal structure available. \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\) is well-defined. BBFlow can condition on it.

IDP

Flat/rugged energy landscape. No crystal structure. AlphaFold produces low-confidence predictions. Any choice of \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\) is wrong.

The problem with using AlphaFold predictions as x_eq: AlphaFold assigns low pLDDT (<50) to disordered regions, indicating its predictions are not physically meaningful conformations. Conditioning BBFlow on these "structures" would bias the ensemble toward an artifact.

BBFlow's conditional prior \(p_0(\mathbf{x}|{\mathbf{x}_{eq}})\) would collapse near this arbitrary structure, defeating the purpose of ensemble generation.

IDP energy landscape: Eliezer, Curr. Opin. Struct. Biol. 2009; pLDDT and IDP: Ruff & Pappu, J. Mol. Biol. 2021; Alderson & Kay, Nat. Chem. Biol. 2021.

Proposed Work

Central hypothesis: sequence encodes ensemble

The key insight motivating IDPFlow: although IDPs lack a stable backbone geometry, their amino acid sequence encodes rich information about their intrinsic structural preferences — charge patterning, hydrophobicity, and secondary structure propensity all shape the conformational ensemble.

Hypothesis: Sequence-derived features — including pretrained language model embeddings (ESM2), predicted secondary structure propensities, and physicochemical descriptors — can substitute for \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\) as the conditioning signal in a flow matching model, enabling accurate IDP ensemble prediction without any structural input.

Formally, we replace the conditional distribution

\[p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{x}_{eq}) \;\longrightarrow\; p(\mathbf{x}|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{seq}})\]

where \(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{seq}}\) is a learned sequence representation that captures the IDP's intrinsic preferences without requiring a stable structure.

ESM2 protein language model: Lin et al. Science 2023; secondary structure prediction: Klausen et al., 2019 (NetSurf-2); physicochemical descriptors: Pappu et al. 2023.

Specific Aims

Three aims toward a sequence-only IDP ensemble model

The proposed work is structured around three specific aims that build from model development to rigorous validation to disease-relevant application.

1

Architecture: Design and train IDPFlow — an SE(3) flow matching model conditioned on ESM2 sequence embeddings and predicted secondary structure propensities, using the IDRome dataset (28,058 trajectories).

2

Validation: Benchmark IDPFlow against experimental IDP ensembles from the PED database and CALVADOS2 MD trajectories, using Rg distributions, RMSF, contact maps, and pairwise RMSD as metrics.

3

Application: Generate and analyze ensembles for disease-relevant IDPs — p53 N-terminal TAD and α-synuclein — and identify transient binding sites using ensemble-based pocket detection.

NSF Specific Aims structure following NIH/NSF guidelines for Molecular and Cellular Biosciences (MCB) program.

Aim 1

Sequence-conditioned flow matching

We will develop IDPFlow by replacing the geometry-based conditioning of BBFlow with a sequence-based conditioning module \(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{seq}}\).

Step 1 — Sequence encoding: We use ESM2 (Lin et al. 2023), a 650M-parameter protein language model, to produce per-residue embeddings. These capture evolutionary co-variation and chemical context without requiring any structural input.

Step 2 — Structural propensity: A lightweight prediction head (or NetSurf-2) outputs helix, strand, and coil propensities per residue, which we append to the ESM2 embeddings.

Step 3 — Conditional prior: Instead of interpolating toward \(\mathbf{x}_{eq}\), we bias the prior using predicted compactness (Rg estimate from sequence) to initialize the flow closer to the target distribution.

\[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{seq}} = [\mathbf{e}_{ESM2}\,;\,\mathbf{p}_{SS}\,;\,f_{phys}]\]
ESM2: Lin et al. Science 2023; NetSurf-2: Klausen et al. Nature Comput. Sci. 2019; FrameFlow/SE(3) flow: Yim et al. 2023; IDRome: Tesei et al. Nature 2024.

Aim 2

Training on the human IDP universe — and validating rigorously

Training data: The IDRome dataset (Tesei et al., 2024) provides coarse-grained CALVADOS2 MD trajectories for 28,058 human IDPs sampled from UniProt. This is the first dataset large enough for sequence-to-ensemble ML training.

Data split: 23,000 training / 2,500 validation / 2,558 test, stratified by sequence identity (MMseqs2 at 30% threshold) to prevent leakage.

Validation metrics (mirroring BBFlow):

  • Rg distribution: Wasserstein-2 distance to MD ensemble
  • RMSF per residue: Pearson r and MAE against MD
  • Contact frequency maps: Frobenius distance
  • Pairwise Cα RMSD distribution: MAE
Out-of-distribution test: 50 IDP sequences not present in IDRome, validated against PED database NMR ensembles (Lazar et al. 2021).
IDRome: Tesei et al. Nature 2024; PED database: Lazar et al. NAR 2021; CALVADOS2: Löhr et al. JCTC 2022; MMseqs2: Steinegger & Söding 2017.

Aim 3

Predicting ensembles of disease-relevant IDPs

p53 N-terminal TAD (residues 1–73): The p53 tumor suppressor has a fully disordered N-terminal activation domain that must form a transient α-helix (Box 1, residues 19–26) to bind the MDM2 oncoprotein. Disrupting this interaction reactivates p53 in cancer cells. IDPFlow will predict the helix population and the transiently exposed binding surface without any structural input.

α-synuclein (full 140 residues): The natively disordered α-synuclein aggregates into amyloid fibrils in Parkinson's disease. The non-amyloid-β component (NAC, residues 61–95) drives aggregation. Experimental evidence suggests transient long-range contacts between the N-terminus and the NAC region inhibit aggregation. IDPFlow will quantify this contact frequency across the ensemble.

Pocket detection: For each disease IDP we will generate 1,000 ensemble samples, cluster them (k=20 centroids), and apply fpocket to each centroid. Pockets absent in any single structure but recurrent across the ensemble are classified as cryptic binding sites — potential drug targets.
p53 TAD transient helix: Raj & Bhatt, eLife 2022; α-synuclein ensemble: Bhatt et al. Nat. Struct. Mol. Biol. 2021; cryptic pockets: Oleinikovas et al. JACS 2016; fpocket: Le Guilloux et al. 2009.

Preliminary Data

Published evidence makes the proposal feasible

The proposal does not require inventing a new data source. It combines three already-demonstrated ingredients into a new IDP-specific model:

  • IDRome: 28,058 human IDP trajectories from CALVADOS2 coarse-grained MD.
  • Sequence physics: charge, hydrophobicity, and charge patterning govern IDP compactness.
  • BBFlow: SE(3) flow matching can learn protein backbone ensembles from MD data.
  • ESM2: protein language embeddings provide per-residue sequence context at scale.

Our preliminary position is therefore evidence-based: if sequence determines IDP ensemble statistics and BBFlow learns MD-like backbone distributions, then replacing \(x_{eq}\) with \(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{seq}}\) is a testable and well-motivated next step.

Preliminary data used here: published IDRome trajectories, BBFlow ATLAS results, and established IDP sequence-property relationships. New regression and ablation results are part of Aim 1.
Feasibility evidence: IDRome, Tesei et al. Nature 2024; CALVADOS2, Loehr et al. JCTC; BBFlow, Wolf et al. 2025; ESM2, Lin et al. Science 2023.

Innovation

IDPFlow: what is genuinely new

IDPFlow advances the field in three distinct ways that distinguish it from all prior work.

1

First sequence-only flow matching model for IDPs. No crystal structure, no MSA, no evolutionary information required. Applicable to any protein sequence — including synthetic and de novo IDPs with no evolutionary record.

2

Scale of training. Training on 28,058 IDP trajectories (IDRome) represents an order-of-magnitude increase over any prior generative model applied to disordered proteins, enabling genuine generalization across sequence space.

3

Disease-relevant outputs. By coupling ensemble prediction to pocket detection, IDPFlow provides a direct path from sequence to drug target hypothesis — a pipeline that does not exist for IDPs today.

AlphaFlow: Jing et al. NeurIPS 2024; BBFlow: Wolf et al. 2025; MDGen: Arts et al. 2023; CALVADOS2: Löhr et al. JCTC 2022; no prior general sequence-only IDP flow model exists.

Impact & Timeline

Expected outcomes and 2-year roadmap

The proposed project will deliver three concrete scientific outcomes within two years.

Software

IDPFlow model and code released on GitHub under MIT license. Trained model weights publicly available for the community.

Database

Ensemble predictions for 10,000+ human IDRs deposited in a searchable database — expanding the IDRome from MD to ML-generated ensembles.

Drug targets

Cryptic binding site predictions for 20 disease-associated IDPs, including p53 TAD and α-synuclein, as a resource for medicinal chemistry.

IDPFlow will transform how we study the disordered proteome — converting any IDP sequence into a navigable conformational ensemble in under a second.
Timeline targets 2 peer-reviewed publications and 1 public software release. NSF MCB funding mechanism; broader impact includes open-source tools and an IDP ensemble database.
